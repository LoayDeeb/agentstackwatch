## What happened

On October 21, Walmart announced three things meant to let AI agents shop at Walmart and Sam's Club for you ([Walmart](https://corporate.walmart.com/news/2026/10/Making-It-Possible-for-Agents-to-Shop-on-Behalf-of-Customers)):

- **Agent Profiles, launching "in the next month."** Each AI agent you use gets its own profile inside your Walmart account, so its orders show up separately from yours. You choose whether it needs your approval for every purchase or can buy on its own up to a spending limit you set (Walmart's examples: $50, $100 or $200). You can delete a profile at any time ([Walmart](https://corporate.walmart.com/news/2026/10/Making-It-Possible-for-Agents-to-Shop-on-Behalf-of-Customers)).
- **The Walmart Agent Gateway.** Instead of clicking through a website built for people, agents connect over MCP, the standard way agents plug into outside tools. Walmart says it starts as a beta for companies and developers, and that it can help an agent find your "usuals" without handing the agent your full purchase history ([Walmart](https://corporate.walmart.com/news/2026/10/Making-It-Possible-for-Agents-to-Shop-on-Behalf-of-Customers)). Reuters describes it as letting assistants such as ChatGPT, Gemini and Meta's Muse connect directly to search products and complete purchases ([Reuters via Investing.com](https://www.investing.com/news/stock-market-news/walmart-launches-tools-for-ai-agents-to-shop-for-customers-4964210)).
- **Terms for AI agents.** Purchases need your approval by default, and Walmart may ask for extra approval even inside a spending limit. Agents never get your card details and can't add or change payment methods. Walmart's normal returns apply, "including if an agent doesn't act as intended." Agents that break the rules get blocked ([Walmart](https://corporate.walmart.com/news/2026/10/Making-It-Possible-for-Agents-to-Shop-on-Behalf-of-Customers)).

Walmart also says agents see the same prices a person sees on Walmart.com ([Walmart](https://corporate.walmart.com/news/2026/10/Making-It-Possible-for-Agents-to-Shop-on-Behalf-of-Customers)), and you don't need a Walmart+ membership ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/10/21/while-amazon-blocks-ai-agents-walmart-rolls-out-the-welcome-mat/)).

**Amazon is going the other way.** TechCrunch reports that Amazon recently began blocking AI agents from buying on behalf of shoppers, and that some travel and booking sites lock agents out entirely ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/10/21/while-amazon-blocks-ai-agents-walmart-rolls-out-the-welcome-mat/)).

**Still unknown:** the exact Agent Profiles launch date, which personal agents are in the Gateway beta (Walmart names no list; Grok Bot, Claude and ChatGPT dots aren't mentioned anywhere we read), and whether it works outside the US.

## Where the big personal agents stand on buying things

- **ChatGPT.** ChatGPT Work's browser can finish tasks on sites you sign in to and "will always ask for confirmation before consequential actions, such as completing a reservation or payment" (Plus and Pro). ChatGPT can also book restaurant tables through OpenTable, Resy and Yelp ([ChatGPT release notes](https://help.openai.com/en/articles/6825453-chatgpt-release-notes)).
- **Grok Bot.** Purchases show a card with the amount for you to Allow or Deny, and Bots can pay with X Money or Stripe Link; at checkout they can use card details you send in chat ([xAI changelog](https://x.ai/changelog/bot)).
- **Gemini and Muse.** Walmart lists both among its existing shopping integrations, alongside ChatGPT ([Walmart](https://corporate.walmart.com/news/2026/10/Making-It-Possible-for-Agents-to-Shop-on-Behalf-of-Customers)).

## Our take

This is the first big retailer to write down rules for agents instead of just blocking them, and the rules are the right ones: a separate profile, a hard spending cap, no card details in the agent's hands, and returns that cover agent mistakes. That's exactly what has been missing when you give an assistant your login today.

**Who should care:** busy households that reorder the same groceries every week, and founders running online stores, because "can an agent buy from you?" is now a question customers will ask.

**What to do now:**

1. **Shoppers:** when Agent Profiles arrive, start with "approve every purchase," then move to a small limit ($50) only after a few weeks of correct orders. Don't give any agent your full Walmart (or Amazon) password in the meantime.
2. **Store owners:** check whether your own shop blocks agents or lets them check out, and decide on purpose. Walmart's terms (approval by default, spending limits, no card access) are a good template for your own policy.
3. **Everyone:** pick an assistant that asks before it pays. ChatGPT Work and Grok Bot both document a confirm-before-paying step; for anything else, test with a cheap order first.
