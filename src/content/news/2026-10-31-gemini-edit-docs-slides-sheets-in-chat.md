## What happened

**1. Edit files inside the Gemini chat (all users).** On **October 29**, Google said you can now "generate, preview, and edit files right within your chat" in the Gemini app. You can expand a file to full screen and type edits yourself, highlight text to ask questions about it, or ask Gemini to refine it. It covers documents, PDFs, presentations, spreadsheets and code, and Google says it's "available to all users in all languages where Gemini app is available" ([Google](https://blog.google/innovation-and-ai/products/gemini-app/edit-files-directly/)).

**The catch:** PDF preview and editing works on the web and on mobile, but everything else (Google Docs, Google Sheets, slide decks, Markdown, TXT files and code) is **web only** for now ([Google](https://blog.google/innovation-and-ai/products/gemini-app/edit-files-directly/)).

Gemini could already create files; GSMArena notes it gained that in April, but until now you had to download or export a file to change it ([GSMArena](https://www.gsmarena.com/gemini_now_lets_you_edit_the_files_it_creates_right_in_the_chat-news-75277.php)). According to 9to5Google, you can preview a slide deck in the chat, then open it in Google Slides or download it as a PPTX or PDF; build a tracker or table and open it in Google Sheets; and upload the files you make to Drive ([9to5Google](https://9to5google.com/2026/10/29/gemini-design-file-generation/)).

**2. Rewrite a whole deck from one prompt in Google Slides (paid plans).** Separately, Gemini in Google Slides can now edit multiple slides or an entire presentation at once from the side panel: refresh fonts and colors across the deck, match the style of another presentation, update content using your Drive files, or condense slides (Google's example: "Summarize slides 5–7 into a single slide"). Gemini decides which slides need changing, edits your real, editable slides and shows a summary of what it changed. You can undo or use version history to roll back ([Google Workspace Updates](https://workspaceupdates.googleblog.com/2026/10/edit-entire-presentations-with-gemini-in-Google-Slides.html)).

**Who gets the Slides feature:** Workspace Business Standard and Plus, Enterprise Standard and Plus, Google AI Pro and Ultra, and Google AI Pro for Education. It's English only at launch, and the rollout started October 29 and may take more than 15 days to reach everyone. Workspace customers get higher promotional limits for multi-slide edits through at least December 1, 2026, then per-user limits apply ([Google Workspace Updates](https://workspaceupdates.googleblog.com/2026/10/edit-entire-presentations-with-gemini-in-Google-Slides.html)).

Google's weekly Workspace recap also says the AI function in Google Sheets ("Fill with Gemini") now shows the web sources it used inside each cell it fills in ([Google Workspace Updates](https://workspaceupdates.googleblog.com/2026/10/weekly-recap-10-30-2026.html)).

## Our take

**This is the boring upgrade that saves the most time.** For most people the hard part of using an AI assistant at work isn't the first draft. It's the back and forth: download, fix a line, re-upload, ask again. Editing in place removes that loop, and it's on the **free** Gemini app, not just paid plans. For comparison, ChatGPT's new Slides feature is only for Pro, Business and Enterprise ([our Oct 23 news post](/news/2026-10-23-chatgpt-locked-chats-slides-pro-agents/)).

**For founders, sales and marketing teams:** the Slides update is the one to try first. "Make this deck match our brand template" and "update these numbers from this Drive file" are jobs that eat an afternoon before a pitch or a quarterly review. Two things to do: give it a reference deck rather than describing your brand in words, and read the change summary before you send anything, because it edits your real slides. Version history is your safety net. If you're picking tools for content work, see our [best AI agent for marketing](/best-ai-agent-for-marketing/) roundup.

**For everyday users:** use the desktop browser for now. On your phone you only get PDF editing. A good first test is something you'd otherwise do in Word or Sheets, such as a packing list, a budget tracker or a one-page CV, then open it in Google Docs or Sheets when it's close.

**Is it an agent?** Not really. These features act when you ask, on the file or deck in front of you. But it's the piece that makes the agent's output usable.
