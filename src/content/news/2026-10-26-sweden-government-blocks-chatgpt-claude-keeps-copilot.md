## What happened

Sweden's Government Offices (Regeringskansliet, the body that supports the prime minister and the ministries) is switching off browser access to **ChatGPT and Claude** on staff computers from **Monday, October 26**. Staff are pointed to **Microsoft Copilot** instead, which the Government Offices says it approved after a review and then raised its security settings for ([SVT](https://www.svt.se/nyheter/inrikes/ai-tjanster-sparras-pa-regeringskansliet)). Sveriges Radio's Ekot first reported the change ([Computer Sweden](https://computersweden.se/article/4239687/regeringskansliet-sparrar-chat-gpt-och-claude.html)).

In an email to SVT, the Government Offices said the browser versions of ChatGPT and Claude "will no longer be reachable" from work computers from October 26, but that staff still have access to the ChatGPT and Claude models inside **Intric**, an internal AI platform ([SVT](https://www.svt.se/nyheter/inrikes/ai-tjanster-sparras-pa-regeringskansliet)). So this isn't a ban on the models, it's a ban on the open consumer websites.

The reason given came from digitalisation director Mikael Wettercrantz, who wrote on the staff intranet that using AI services without a contract can have "consequences for our information security" ([Computer Sweden](https://computersweden.se/article/4239687/regeringskansliet-sparrar-chat-gpt-och-claude.html)). Sweden's Tax Agency and the National Board of Institutional Care already have similar blocks, and the Enforcement Authority is considering one ([SVT](https://www.svt.se/nyheter/inrikes/ai-tjanster-sparras-pa-regeringskansliet)).

**Still unknown:** the Government Offices hasn't said whether Anthropic's Claude models, which Microsoft also offers inside Copilot, are switched on in its Copilot setup. In the EU they're off by default and an admin has to turn them on ([Notebookcheck](https://www.notebookcheck.net/Sweden-s-government-blocks-ChatGPT-and-Claude-keeps-Copilot.1433522.0.html)). It also hasn't said how many staff can use Intric ([Decrypt](https://decrypt.co/381763/sweden-government-offices-block-chatgpt-claude)).

## Our take

**This is a contract decision, not a "which AI is best" decision.** Copilot won because the Government Offices has an agreement with Microsoft and control over the settings. The same models are still allowed through an internal platform. If you run a team, that's the useful lesson: the risk people worry about is staff pasting company information into personal, free or consumer accounts where nobody at the company controls what happens to it.

**Your company will face the same question, just smaller.** Most small teams already use AI on a mix of personal ChatGPT, Claude and Gemini accounts. That's fine until someone pastes in a customer list, a contract or payroll numbers. You don't need a ban. You need a simple rule:

1. **Pick one or two approved tools and pay for the business plan.** ChatGPT, Claude and Gemini all have team or business plans that give you an admin and a contract. Our [ChatGPT vs Claude for business](/chatgpt-vs-claude-for-business/) guide compares them.
2. **Write down what never goes into a personal account:** customer data, contracts, passwords, anything under NDA.
3. **Turn on only the connections people need.** An agent that can read your inbox and drive is far more useful and far more sensitive than a chat window.

**For everyday users this changes nothing,** unless you work for a government or a big company. If you do, expect more employers to steer you to one approved assistant on work devices. Keep your personal agent for personal stuff and don't log into it with work data.

**One more thing worth noticing:** a government steering staff to Microsoft is a reminder of how much distribution matters. Copilot doesn't have to win the "best assistant" race inside companies that already run on Microsoft. It just has to be the one IT has already signed for.
