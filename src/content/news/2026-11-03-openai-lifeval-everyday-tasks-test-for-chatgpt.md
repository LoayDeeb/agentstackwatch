## What happened

On **November 2, 2026**, OpenAI published **LifeVal**, a new test that measures how well its models handle "the tasks that matter in everyday life" ([OpenAI](https://openai.com/index/lifeval)).

OpenAI says it built the task list from two big studies of how people spend their time, the American Time Use Survey and the Multinational Time Use Study, plus its own survey of **3,995 people** about where they'd most want a personal assistant's help. The tasks cover caregiving, health, education, household admin, personal finances, shopping and dealing with government services ([OpenAI](https://openai.com/index/lifeval)).

The post shows example tasks with scores for OpenAI's current models, including:

- Shortlisting kindergartens after a move: **93.1%**
- Organising neighbourhood volunteers before a flood: **98.0%**
- Finding a family home that fits a budget and commute, then adding the deed to a records cart for review: **100%**
- Setting up a way to watch a game remotely with grandma: **96.3%**
- A first-week care and meal plan for a parent newly diagnosed with diabetes: **90.4%**

OpenAI says it used LifeVal over the past year to push its models on browser use, computer use and connected apps, to move ChatGPT "beyond a chat tool and towards a truly agentic personal assistant" ([OpenAI](https://openai.com/index/lifeval)).

## Our take

**This is OpenAI telling you what ChatGPT is for next: errands.** Not trivia, not essays. Booking, comparing, filling in forms and keeping a plan moving. That's the same race Claude Duet, Gemini and Grok Bot are in, and it's good news for anyone who just wants the boring stuff handled.

**But treat the numbers as a brochure, not a review.** OpenAI picked the tasks, ran the test and graded itself. The public post shows example scores for its own models; in what we read, it doesn't put rival assistants on the same test. A 100% on one sample task tells you it *can* work, not that it will work on your messy inbox.

**For founders and business leaders:**

- **Steal the method, not the score.** Write down the 10 chores your team actually repeats each week (chasing invoices, booking travel, prepping client calls). Run each one through two assistants and see which finishes without you babysitting it. That's your own LifeVal, and it's the only one that matters for your money.
- **Notice where the "review" step sits.** In OpenAI's own examples the assistant stops and asks before the final step ("Want to review the shortlist?"). Pick tools that pause before paying, sending or signing anything.

**For everyday users:** this is the right direction. Try ChatGPT on one real errand this week, like a weekly meal plan or comparing two phone plans, and check every number it gives you before you act on it. If it's health or money, the assistant drafts and you decide.
