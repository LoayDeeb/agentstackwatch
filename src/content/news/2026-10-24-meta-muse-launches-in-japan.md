## What happened

On October 23, 2026 (Japan time), Meta launched **Muse**, its personal AI agent, in Japan. Meta says Japan is the first place it's available outside North America, where it launched in September ([Meta Japan newsroom](https://about.fb.com/ja/news/2026/10/muse-personal-ai-agent-launches-in-japan/), in Japanese). Muse came to the US on September 8 ([Reuters](https://www.reuters.com/business/meta-launches-ai-agent-that-can-access-other-apps-send-emails-make-payments-2026-09-08/)), and Japan's Kyodo News also reported the launch as Muse's first market outside North America ([Kyodo News](https://english.kyodonews.net/articles/-/89228)).

### What Japanese users get

- **Where:** the iOS and Android apps and the web at muse.ai. Many basic features are free, and there are subscription plans for heavier use ([Meta Japan newsroom](https://about.fb.com/ja/news/2026/10/muse-personal-ai-agent-launches-in-japan/)).
- **How it works:** you message Muse like you'd message a person. It plans the job, then works on its own: it can open a browser and fill in forms for you, keeps going on long tasks after you close the app, and pings you when something changes or it needs your approval to send an email or buy something. It remembers what matters to you and uses past conversations ([Meta Japan newsroom](https://about.fb.com/ja/news/2026/10/muse-personal-ai-agent-launches-in-japan/)).
- **Apps it connects to:** Gmail, Google Calendar and Instagram, plus global services including Stripe, Shopify, Trip.com, Agoda and Klook for shopping and travel ([Meta Japan newsroom](https://about.fb.com/ja/news/2026/10/muse-personal-ai-agent-launches-in-japan/)). ITmedia's example from the release: ask it to find a hotel for next month's Osaka trip under ¥30,000, and it checks your Google Calendar, compares places on the web and fills in the booking form ([ITmedia](https://www.itmedia.co.jp/news/article/2610/23/2000002535/)).
- **Local partners on the way:** Meta says it's working on connections with the flea-market app Mercari, the budgeting app Money Forward ME and Ticket Pia. These were announced as "in progress", not live ([Meta Japan newsroom](https://about.fb.com/ja/news/2026/10/muse-personal-ai-agent-launches-in-japan/)).

### The safety setup

- Each user's Muse runs in its own isolated cloud computer, which Meta calls the **Muse Secure VM**, where connected-app data and logins are stored.
- A separate agent called **Sentinel** controls Muse's internet access.
- Muse **can't see your passwords or payment details**, and it asks you before important actions like sending an email or buying something. You can see a history of what it has done and what it plans to do.
- You choose which apps it can reach and can disconnect any time. You can opt out of Meta using your Muse chats to train its AI, and Meta says your chats and the data in the virtual machine aren't shared with its ad systems ([Meta Japan newsroom](https://about.fb.com/ja/news/2026/10/muse-personal-ai-agent-launches-in-japan/)).

### What's not there yet

Meta Connect features such as the advanced voice mode, the Muse Realtime Avatar (English only) and Muse's own email address will have their Japan availability "announced separately" ([Meta Japan newsroom](https://about.fb.com/ja/news/2026/10/muse-personal-ai-agent-launches-in-japan/)). Meta hasn't given dates for any other country, including the UK, the EU or the Middle East, and when we last checked its help center, it described Muse as "in limited testing" and not available everywhere ([Meta help center](https://www.meta.com/help/subscriptions/1021145227643680/)).

## Our take

**The race just went international, and Meta moved first.** Claude Duet is US-only for now, and OpenAI's dots skip Europe, Switzerland and the UK. Meta is now taking Muse country by country, starting with a big non-English market, and with a free tier. For most everyday users outside the US, that matters more than any benchmark.

**Watch the local hook-ups, not the AI.** The interesting part of this launch is Mercari, Money Forward and Ticket Pia. A personal agent is only as useful as the apps it can actually touch, and those are the apps people in Japan use every week. Expect every agent maker to do country-by-country deals like this. If you run a consumer app anywhere, the question to ask now is "how does an agent use my service?"

**The safety design is converging, which is good news.** Muse, Claude Duet and ChatGPT's agents now share the same basics: a sandboxed cloud computer, no access to your passwords or card number, and an approval step before anything that sends or pays. Keep that approval step on, and turn off training on your chats if you care about privacy.
