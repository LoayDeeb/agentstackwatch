## What happened

On **November 3, 2026**, Apple opened **Siri AI** to every developer through a Developer Preview in the iOS 27.3, iPadOS 27.3, macOS 27.3 and visionOS 27.3 betas ([Apple Developer](https://developer.apple.com/news/?id=gdk2umrf)).

Any app can now plug its content and actions into Siri AI using App Intents, so people can ask in their own words and Siri carries out the action, "from checking a delivery to booking a reservation to placing an order." Apple is also adding **skills**, which teach Siri to chain several actions into one request ([Apple Developer](https://developer.apple.com/news/?id=gdk2umrf)).

A few details matter:

- It's a **preview**. Apple says it ships to customers "in a few months," with App Store submissions opening in the spring.
- Apple's own apps (Calendar, Mail, Maps, Messages, Notes, Photos, Reminders, Safari and more) are already getting these actions.
- Siri AI isn't available in the EU on iPhone, iPad or Apple Watch for now, and server-side features have daily usage limits, with Apple saying expanded access "will be available for a fee in the future" ([Apple Developer](https://developer.apple.com/news/?id=gdk2umrf)).

The same day, Apple filed its annual report. In the risk section it says AI agents "may behave in unintended or unpredictable ways, including by taking actions that were not directed, instructed or authorized, exceeding permissions, defying directions or resisting instructions," and that some consequences, "such as deleting data or completing transactions, may be difficult or impossible to reverse" ([Apple 10-K, SEC](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/320193/000032019326000026/aapl-20260926.htm); reported by [CNBC](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/11/03/apple-warns-of-ai-agents-behave-in-unintended-or-unpredictable-ways.html)).

## Our take

**This is the moment the personal agent lands on a billion phones.** ChatGPT, Claude, Gemini and Grok Bot all want to be the assistant that does your errands. Apple's edge is that it already sits on your phone, next to your apps. Once your bank, airline and grocery apps plug in, "Siri, reorder last week's groceries" stops being a demo.

**Read the warning as honesty, not panic.** Risk sections are lawyers listing everything that could go wrong. But it's telling that the company shipping an agent to everyone spells out, in plain words, that agents can overstep and that some mistakes can't be undone. Every assistant has this problem. Apple just wrote it down.

**For founders and business leaders:**

- **If customers use your app on iPhone, this is a new front door.** Whoever's app works cleanly with Siri gets picked when someone says "book me a table" or "where's my order." Ask your team or agency now what it would take to expose your top two or three actions. You have until spring before the App Store opens to it.
- **Make the risky steps ask first.** Payments, cancellations and anything that sends a message should need a "yes" from the user. Apple's warning is a free checklist for your own agent projects too.

**For everyday users:** nothing changes on your phone today unless you run betas. When it arrives, start with low-stakes asks like finding things, reminders and tracking deliveries. Keep confirmations on for anything that spends money or sends something in your name.
